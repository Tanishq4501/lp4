## 1. What the PS asks (in plain words)
Train a CBOW model on the short text `document3.txt`: prepare the text, train, and evaluate with example words (nearest words and similarity).

## 2. Dataset
- `document3.txt`: a tiny text (about 190 words, 8 sentences) about viruses (virus, influenza, covid, transmission, symptoms).
- After stop-word removal the vocabulary is **61 words**.

## 3. Notebook walkthrough: every cell, and why

### Cell 0: imports
```python
import numpy as np
import re
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from gensim.models import Word2Vec
from gensim.utils import simple_preprocess
from gensim.parsing.preprocessing import remove_stopwords
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
```
**Why:** `gensim` provides `Word2Vec`; `simple_preprocess` lowercases and splits text into words; `remove_stopwords` deletes very common words; `numpy`/`pandas` hold the data and `matplotlib` plots. `re` splits the text into sentences; `PCA` makes the map.

### Cell 1: read the text and make sentences
```python
tw = open("document3.txt",encoding="UTF-8").read()
sents = [simple_preprocess(remove_stopwords(t.lower())) for t in re.split(r"[.!?]",tw)]
sents = [s for s in sents if s]
```
**Why:** `re.split(r"[.!?]", text)` cuts the text at sentence ends; every sentence is lowercased, stop words removed and tokenised; empty sentences are dropped. Word2Vec trains on a **list of sentences** (each a list of words).

**Why not the alternative:** Not one giant token list: sentence boundaries matter for the context window.

### Cell 2: train CBOW with tiny-text settings
```python
wv = Word2Vec(sents,vector_size=50,window=3,min_count=1,sg=0,epochs=100,workers=1).wv
```
**Why:** `vector_size=50`, `window=3`, `min_count=1`, `sg=0` (CBOW), `epochs=100`, `workers=1`. The text is tiny, so: every word must be kept (`min_count=1`), the vectors are small, the context window is short, and the model needs many passes (100). `workers=1` avoids needless multithreading on such a small corpus.

**Why not the alternative:** Not the 4B settings (`min_count=2`, 30 epochs): too few words and too little training. Not 300 epochs: on a tiny text the vectors collapse and all become nearly identical.

### Cell 3: vocabulary size
```python
print("vocabulary:",len(wv))
```
**Why:** `len(wv)` = 61 words that received a vector.

**Why not the alternative:** Not counting raw words: stop words were removed.

### Cell 4: nearest words
```python
print(wv.most_similar("virus",topn=5))
```
**Why:** `most_similar("virus", topn=5)` returns the words closest to `virus`: symptoms 0.53, covid 0.52, prior 0.49, appearance 0.48, hours 0.47.

**Why not the alternative:** Not a word outside the vocabulary (`KeyError`); `virus` occurs in the text.

### Cell 5: similarity of two words
```python
print(wv.similarity("virus","influenza"))
```
**Why:** `similarity("virus", "influenza")` = 0.29: both occur in similar sentences but the text is tiny, so the number is modest.

**Why not the alternative:** Not expecting a high value from 190 words.

### Cell 6: 2-D map
```python
words = wv.index_to_key[:30]
xy = PCA(2).fit_transform(wv[words])                   # 2-D map of the words
plt.scatter(*xy.T)
[plt.annotate(a, p) for a, p in zip(words, xy)]
plt.show()      # close points = related words
```
**Why:** PCA of the 30 most frequent word vectors, annotated; nearby points are related words.

**Why not the alternative:** Not t-SNE: PCA is simple and deterministic.

## 4. Terminology used in this notebook

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Word embedding** | A dense vector (for example 100 numbers) for a word; words used in similar contexts get similar vectors. |
| **Word2Vec** | An algorithm (Mikolov, 2013) that learns word embeddings from a prediction task on text; gensim implements it. |
| **CBOW** | Continuous Bag of Words (`sg=0`): the averaged context words predict the centre word. Fast, good for frequent words. |
| **window (Word2Vec)** | How many words on each side count as context (5 for tweets, 3 for the tiny document). |
| **vector_size** | The length of each word vector (100 or 50). |
| **min_count** | Words rarer than this are ignored (2 for tweets, 5 for IMDB, 1 for the tiny document). |
| **Stop words** | Very common words ('the', 'and') that carry little meaning and are removed. |
| **Tokenisation** | Splitting text into words (`simple_preprocess` also lowercases and drops punctuation). |
| **Vocabulary** | The set of distinct words that received a vector. |
| **Cosine similarity** | The cosine of the angle between two vectors: (a . b) divided by (length of a x length of b). 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated. |
| **PCA** | Principal component analysis: projects high-dimensional vectors to 2-D for plotting while keeping as much spread as possible. |

## 5. Theory you must be able to explain

**Same model as 4B, different data size.** The parameters must follow the data: a tiny text needs `min_count=1`, a short window and many epochs.

**Why 300 epochs breaks it.** With only a few hundred word occurrences the network over-trains and pulls every vector to the same point, so similarities become meaningless (all near 1).

**Reading the numbers.** `virus` is nearest to words that share its sentences (symptoms, covid). A score of 0.5 on 190 words is a weak but visible relation.

**Evaluation.** Pick example words, list their nearest words and compare pairs with `similarity`; the PCA map is a visual summary.

## 6. Why we did this, and why not that

| Decision | Why we did it | Why not the alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Split into sentences | Context windows should not cross sentence ends | One long list mixes sentences |
| `min_count=1` | Every word counts in a tiny text | Default 5 would delete almost everything |
| 100 epochs, `vector_size=50`, `window=3` | Small data needs more passes and smaller vectors | 300 epochs makes vectors identical |
| Qualitative evaluation | No labels | Accuracy does not apply |

## 8. Mistakes and traps (from your own notebooks)
- Use `min_count=1` and about 100 epochs on the tiny text.
- Query only words in the vocabulary.
- Lowercase before removing stop words (otherwise 'The' stays as 'the').

## 9. Viva questions with answers

**Q. Why `min_count=1` here?**

A. The text is tiny; with a higher value almost every word would be dropped.

**Q. Why 100 epochs?**

A. A tiny corpus needs many passes to learn; 300 collapses the vectors.

**Q. Why split into sentences?**

A. Word2Vec uses sentences as context units.

**Q. Why is the similarity only 0.29?**

A. Too little text to learn a strong relation.

**Q. Why `workers=1`?**

A. A tiny corpus gains nothing from threads and results are more repeatable.

**Q. Skip-gram instead?**

A. It could be better for rare words on small data, but the PS asks for CBOW (`sg=0`).
