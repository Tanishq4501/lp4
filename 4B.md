## 1. What the PS asks (in plain words)
Collect and pre-process the tweets, train a CBOW model, and evaluate the embeddings (nearest words, similarity, the word vectors, a 2-D map).

## 2. Dataset
- `twitter_dataset.csv`: **10,000 tweets** with `Tweet_ID`, `Username`, `Text`, `Retweets`, `Likes`, `Timestamp`.
- The text is **machine-generated random words**, so there is no real meaning to find: similarities will be low and arbitrary. This is expected.

## 3. Notebook walkthrough: every cell, and why

### Cell 0: imports
```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from gensim.models import Word2Vec
from gensim.utils import simple_preprocess
from gensim.parsing.preprocessing import remove_stopwords
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
```
**Why:** `gensim` provides `Word2Vec`; `simple_preprocess` lowercases and splits text into words; `remove_stopwords` deletes very common words; `numpy`/`pandas` hold the data and `matplotlib` plots. `PCA` projects the vectors to 2-D for the map.

### Cell 1: load the tweets
```python
tw = pd.read_csv("twitter_dataset.csv")
tw.head()
```
**Why:** Reads the CSV; `tw.head()` shows the `Text` column we use.

### Cell 2: pre-process and train CBOW
```python
tok = [simple_preprocess(remove_stopwords(t.lower())) for t in tw.Text]
wv = Word2Vec(tok,vector_size=100,window=5,min_count=2,sg=0,epochs=30).wv
```
**Why:** `remove_stopwords(t.lower())` lowercases first (the stop-word list is lowercase) and removes common words; `simple_preprocess` splits into words. `Word2Vec(tok, vector_size=100, window=5, min_count=2, sg=0, epochs=30)`: 100-number vectors, context of 5 words each side, ignore words seen once, **CBOW**, 30 passes (the corpus is small, so more epochs help). `.wv` keeps only the word vectors. Vocabulary: 969 words.

**Why not the alternative:** Not skip-gram. Not `min_count=5`: with this small corpus too many words would disappear. Not training on one tweet at a time: the model needs all tweets as context.

### Cell 3: ask the embeddings
```python
w = wv.index_to_key[:3]
print(w, "| vocabulary:", len(wv))
print(wv.most_similar(w[0], topn=5))                                                 # nearest words
print(wv.similarity(w[0], w[1]))                                                     # similarity of two words (-1..1)
print(wv[w[0]][:5])                                                                  # the vector of a word
```
**Why:** `w` holds the 3 most frequent words (always in the vocabulary, so no `KeyError`). `most_similar` lists the nearest words, `similarity` gives the cosine similarity of two words (-1 to 1) and `wv[w[0]][:5]` shows the first five numbers of a word's vector. Your result: `hard` is nearest to page 0.32, cut 0.32, community 0.28; similarity(`hard`, `tax`) = 0.03.

**Why not the alternative:** Not accuracy: embeddings have no label to score. They are evaluated qualitatively.

### Cell 4: 2-D map of the words
```python
words = wv.index_to_key[:30]
xy = PCA(2).fit_transform(wv[words])                   # 2-D map of the words
plt.scatter(*xy.T)
[plt.annotate(a, p) for a, p in zip(words, xy)]
plt.show()      # close points = related words
```
**Why:** `PCA(2)` projects the 100-number vectors of the 30 most frequent words to 2-D and each point is annotated with its word; close points are related words. With random text the map shows no real clusters.

**Why not the alternative:** Not t-SNE: PCA is deterministic and simple.

## 4. Terminology used in this notebook

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Word embedding** | A dense vector (for example 100 numbers) for a word; words used in similar contexts get similar vectors. |
| **Word2Vec** | An algorithm (Mikolov, 2013) that learns word embeddings from a prediction task on text; gensim implements it. |
| **CBOW** | Continuous Bag of Words (`sg=0`): the averaged context words predict the centre word. Fast, good for frequent words. |
| **Skip-gram** | `sg=1`: the centre word predicts each context word. Slower, better for rare words. |
| **window (Word2Vec)** | How many words on each side count as context (5 for tweets, 3 for the tiny document). |
| **vector_size** | The length of each word vector (100 or 50). |
| **min_count** | Words rarer than this are ignored (2 for tweets, 5 for IMDB, 1 for the tiny document). |
| **Stop words** | Very common words ('the', 'and') that carry little meaning and are removed. |
| **Tokenisation** | Splitting text into words (`simple_preprocess` also lowercases and drops punctuation). |
| **Vocabulary** | The set of distinct words that received a vector. |
| **Cosine similarity** | The cosine of the angle between two vectors: (a . b) divided by (length of a x length of b). 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated. |
| **PCA** | Principal component analysis: projects high-dimensional vectors to 2-D for plotting while keeping as much spread as possible. |

## 5. Theory you must be able to explain

**What CBOW learns.** For each position the context words are averaged and used to predict the centre word; the embedding matrix that makes this prediction work becomes the word vectors. Words used in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.

**Parameters.** `vector_size` (dimension), `window` (context size), `min_count` (ignore rare words), `epochs` (passes), `sg` (0 CBOW, 1 skip-gram). Gensim uses negative sampling (5 negatives by default) instead of a full softmax over the vocabulary.

**Why your scores are low.** The tweets are random words, so no word predicts another. Scores of 0.25-0.32 are what random text gives. On real text (IMDB in 4A) the nearest words are meaningful (great -> excellent 0.77).

**Cosine similarity.** `(a . b) / (|a| |b|)`: the angle between two vectors, ignoring their length.

**Evaluation without a label.** Nearest words, similarity scores and the 2-D map are the standard qualitative checks (intrinsic evaluation).

## 6. Why we did this, and why not that

| Decision | Why we did it | Why not the alternative |
|---|---|---|
| CBOW (`sg=0`) | Required by the PS; fast | Skip-gram is the other variant |
| `min_count=2` | Keeps more of the small vocabulary | Higher values would remove too many words |
| 30 epochs | Small corpus needs more passes | 5 epochs under-trains |
| Lowercase before stop-word removal | The list is lowercase | Capitalised stop words would stay |
| Qualitative evaluation | Embeddings have no label | Accuracy is only available for a downstream task (4A) |
